Camera Calibrate Pro

Your phone as the calibration target

Camera Calibrate Pro turns an iPhone into a calibration target for a head-worn stereo camera. The screen shows AprilTag markers drawn exactly to its pixels, so their size is known to the millimetre, and a QR code telling the calibration software which phone and scale it is looking at. You hold the phone in front of the head cameras while they record; the recording is calibrated afterwards with the rig's offline software.

How to calibrate

  1. Start the recording on the headset.
  2. Open the app, tap Start calibration and confirm the headset is recording.
  3. Hold the phone with the screen facing you and do what the screen says, one step at a time: arm straight, elbow bent, close to your face, then tilt the phone to each side and top toward and away from you.
  4. After about a minute the app says All done. Stop the headset recording.

Each step shows a countdown and ends with a green tick. The markers change size during each step on purpose. The tilt parts complete by themselves when the phone has turned far enough.

Questions

Do I need Do Not Disturb? No. A notification banner only covers the instructions at the top of the screen, never the markers. If a call or alarm takes over the screen, the interrupted step is simply done again.

How does the app know the size of the markers? From the phone model's published pixel density. Once per phone model you can check it with a ruler: on the home screen, Check scale with a ruler draws a 100 mm bar; type the length you measure and the app uses it from then on.

Why does the app use the front camera? Only to measure how far the phone is from your face, so it can say "a bit closer" or "a bit farther". No image is stored or sent.

Where is my data? On your phone only. Each calibration writes a session log (the phone's motion, its distance from your face, and what was shown when) that leaves the phone only if you share it. See the privacy policy.

Which phones work? iPhones with iOS 17 or later. Distance hints need a Face ID iPhone; without it the calibration still works.
